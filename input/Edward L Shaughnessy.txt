Edward Louis Shaughnessy
(born July 29, 1952) is an American
sinologist
, scholar, and educator, known for his studies of early
Chinese history
, particularly the
Zhou dynasty
, and his studies of the
Classic of Changes
(
I Ching
易經)
.
[
1
]
Life and career
[
edit
]
Edward Shaughnessy was born on July 29, 1952.  He attended the
University of Notre Dame
as an undergraduate student, graduating in 1974 with a
B.A.
in
theology
, after which he spent several years studying
Chinese
in
Taiwan
and
Japanese
in
Kyoto, Japan
.  He then went to
Stanford University
for graduate study in Asian languages, earning his
Ph.D.
in 1983 with a dissertation entitled "The Composition of the
Zhouyi
". After receiving his Ph.D., Shaughnessy joined the faculty of the Department of East Asian Languages and Civilizations at the
University of Chicago
, where he is currently the Lorraine J. and
Herrlee G. Creel
Distinguished Service Professor of Early Chinese Studies.
[
2
]
Shaughnessy's wife, Elena Valussi, is an Italian scholar of East Asian history who teaches at
Loyola University Chicago
.  They have two children.
