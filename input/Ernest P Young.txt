Ernest P. Young
is an American historian who focused his research on the politics and international relations of China in the late 19th and early 20th century China. He taught at the
University of Michigan
from 1968 to 2002, and became the Richard Hudson Professor of History in 1998.
Biography
[
edit
]
Young earned an A.B and PhD in History and Far Eastern Languages at
Harvard University
in 1965. Young worked as a professor of oriental history at
Dartmouth College
and as assistant to
Edwin O. Reischauer
, the US Ambassador to Japan.
[
1
]
Young moved to the
University of Michigan
in 1968, was  promoted to professor in 1974, and was appointed the Richard Hudson Professor of History in 1998. He retired in 2002 to become an emeritus professor.
[
2
]
His marriage to foreign policy expert
Marilyn B. Young
, a professor at
New York University
, ended in divorce.
[
3
]
He later remarried M. Brady Mikusko, a life coach and mediator.
[
4
]
Academic career
[
edit
]
Young's publications ranged from
Yuan Shikai
's presidency to the French imperialist project supporting the
Catholic Church
in China, and
Sino-Japanese relations
.
During the
Vietnam War
, his travel to Japan to interview a group of American anti-war deserters known as "The Intrepid Four" made headlines.

1
]
