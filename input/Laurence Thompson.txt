Laurence G. Thompson
(1920 - July 10, 2005) was a World War II veteran,
sinologist
, classical
violinist
and professor emeritus of East Asian languages and cultures at the
University of Southern California
.
Biography
[
edit
]
Thompson was born in 1920 in the
Shandong
province of
Republic of China
, the son of missionaries Kenneth Kilgore Thompson and Denise Archer Thompson. His family had to leave China in 1927 during the unrest of the
Northern Expedition
, when they moved to
Thailand
, where he caught
Malaria
. They returned to China in 1929, and he lived there until age 14, when his father retired.
[
1
]
While in China, he was educated at the Chinan Foreign School and the Shanghai American School.
[
1
]
Back in the
United States
, Thompson completed high school in
Southern California
. In 1942, Thompson earned a bachelor's degree from
UCLA
.
[
1
]
As a young man, he joined the
U.S. Marine Corps
and worked as a Japanese-language interpreter. During
World War II
, he served in the
South Pacific
.
[
1
]
In 1947, he earned a master's degree from
Claremont Graduate School
. Seven years later, he earned a doctorate from Claremont. From 1951 to 1959, Thompson served in the
United States Foreign Service
in
Taipei
,
Tokyo
,
Manila
and
Hong Kong
.
[
1
]
In
Seoul
and Taipei, he was a staff member of
The Asia Foundation
.
[
1
]
Academic career
[
edit
]
An accomplished classical violinist, Thompson taught music at
National Taiwan Normal University
from 1959 to 1962.
[
1
]
He was a
Pomona College
faculty member from 1962 to 1965, and a USC faculty member from 1965 to 1986. At USC, he served as chair of the department of East Asian languages and cultures from 1968 to 1970, and from 1972 to 1976. From 1972 to 1974, he became the founding director of USC East Asian Studies Center.
[
1
]
Research
[
edit
]
Thompson's first major publication was a translation of
Kang Youwei
's "Da Tong Shu," (Ta T'ung Shu) in 1958.
[
2
]
His main intellectual commitment was to his pioneering studies of
Chinese religion
.
[
1
]
[
3
]
He wrote
Chinese Religion: An Introduction
,.
[
1
]
[
4
]
which went through 5 editions,
[
5
]
and that Paper (2012) called "The best introductory study" of Chinese religion,
[
6
]
and
The Chinese Way in Religion
.
[
7
]
His three-volume bibliographical work
Chinese Religions: Publications in Western Languages
(1970) is a basic resource to the field,
[
1
]
which he continued to update in retirement in 1990.
[
8
]
He authored the article on Chinese religion for
Encyclopædia Britannica
, 15th edition.
[
citation needed
]
Also, he served as president of the
Society for the Study of Chinese Religions
for nine years,
[
1
]
and in 1992 was honored with a
festschrift
in the
Journal of Chinese Religions
. In addition, Thompson translated several volumes of religious studies by
Wu Yaoyu
and documents on
Taiwanese
studies.
[
citation needed
]
