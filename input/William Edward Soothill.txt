From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Methodist missionary
William Edward Soothill
,
FRGS
(1861 – 1935) was a Methodist missionary to China who later became
Professor of Chinese
at the
University of Oxford
, and a leading British
sinologist
.
Life
[
edit
]
Born in
Halifax
,
Yorkshire
in January 1861, Soothill matriculated at
London University
.
[
1
]
He entered the ministry of the
United Methodist Free Church
arriving in China in 1882 and spent 29 years as a
missionary
in
Wenzhou
,
China
.
[
2
]
Another leading missionary there until 1909 was
Grace Stott
who led the
China Inland Mission
there.
[
3
]
Soothill founded a hospital, a training college, schools and 200 preaching stations. In 1911 Soothill became President of the
Imperial University at Shanxi
. Upon his return to England in 1920 he was appointed
Professor of Chinese
at Oxford University.
[
2
]
In 1921, he was awarded the
Order of Wen-Hu
(third class) by the  Republic of China in recognition of services rendered in connection with the
Chinese Labour Corps
in France. In 1926 he was a member of
Lord Willingdon
's delegation to China on the settlement of the
Boxer Rebellion
indemnities.
He is best known for his translation into English of the
Analects of Confucius
[
2
]
and his
Dictionary of Chinese
Buddhist
Terms with
Sanskrit
and English Equivalents and a Sanskrit-
Pali
Index
.
He married Lucy Farrar in 1884. She wrote an account of their years in China entitled
A Passport to China
. A daughter was
Dorothea Hosie
, whose husband was the diplomat Sir Alexander Hosie; Lady Hosie was the author of a number of books about China.
[
4
]
[
5
]
John Farrar Soothill
, medical doctor, was a grandson.
